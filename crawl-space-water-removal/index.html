Crawl Space Water Removal in Coeur d'Alene

Water under the house is easy to miss. It tends to turn up as a damp chill underfoot, a stale smell at the vents, or a small lake discovered the day somebody lifts the hatch. By then the insulation is usually soaked and the wood above it has been wet for days. We pump the space dry, remove the ruined material and get the wood dried while it is still sound.

Once the standing water is gone, the job is only halfway done. Because lumber under a floor is slow to soak and slow to dry, our crew tracks its moisture content with a meter and runs dehumidification for the 3 to 5 days it normally takes to bring the readings down. We also look for where the water came from, a leaking supply line, a drain, or groundwater after rain, so it does not fill back up.

Signs to Look For

  • Joists, beams or subfloor panels coated with whitish or blackish fuzz
  • Insulation that has pulled loose and hangs in wet clumps below the joists
  • Wood flooring on the first level that has begun to curl up along the seams
  • Standing water or mud on the ground when you open the access hatch
  • A musty, earthy smell that is strongest near the floor vents
  • Floors above that feel cold, damp, soft or slightly bouncy

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Shifting Piers and Supports

Long periods of standing water loosen the soil that holds up the support piers. Doors that stick and floors that slope upstairs are possible signs.

Air From Below

Because air naturally moves upward from under the home, spores and stale odors in a wet crawl space reach your living areas too.

Rot You Cannot See

Decay tends to begin along the upper edge of the framing or where it meets the sill, out of view from the access opening. We probe the wood to find soft spots.

Termites and Other Pests

Rodents, carpenter ants and termites all seek out moist lumber and soggy ground. Getting moisture levels down takes away a main reason for them to stay.

What Happens if You Wait

First day

Water Pools Below

Low areas fill first while the ground underneath takes on water. Batts begin drawing moisture upward and the air below the house turns humid quickly.

Hours 24 to 48

Early Mold Growth

Growth may begin on wet framing and on the paper backing of the batts. Soaked insulation gains weight and starts to sag off the floor above.

Days 3 through 7

Dampness Rises Indoors

Humid air from below works its way up into the rooms. The lumber goes on taking in moisture, a stale smell spreads and flooring can begin to cup.

After a week

Rot Sets In

Lumber left damp this long starts breaking down. Expect softer framing, rusted fasteners, and a wet environment that draws in termites and similar pests.

How We Fix It

How We Handle Crawl Space Water Removal

1

Safety Check and Inspection

Before a technician goes under the house, we look for live electrical dangers, fallen ductwork and dirty water. After that our team marks out the wet area and records how much moisture the framing and subfloor hold.

2

Remove Standing Water

Submersible pumps and extraction hoses remove the standing water, including the low spots and footing trenches where it collects. While pumping, the crew also cleans off the sediment the water left on the plastic.

3

Remove Wet Materials

We bag up the wet insulation, damaged vapor barrier and loose debris and take it all outside. Where mold has begun or is likely, the bare joists and subfloor get cleaned and an antimicrobial applied.

4

Dry and Verify

Low profile air movers and a dehumidifier run under the house until the wood reaches a normal moisture reading. We recheck with a meter and give you the readings and a prevention plan.
